The Vetting Standard

What happens before any contractor touches your home.

Every Gold Seal contractor on HomeGuard completed this process. It cannot be purchased. It cannot be shortcut. It is the same bar for everyone.

New Jersey Raised the Bar in 2024 — HomeGuard Goes Further

In January 2024, Governor Murphy signed new legislation requiring all NJ home improvement contractors to carry a compliance bond and increased insurance as a condition of registration. That is the state minimum. HomeGuard requires six weeks of structured training, a comprehensive exam, a background check, annual renewal, and ongoing performance monitoring. The state floor is not the HomeGuard standard.

01

Six-week Gold Seal Academy

NJ consumer law, financial management, advanced estimating, client communication, subcontractor management, and scaling a contracting business. Taught by Success Institute faculty.

02

Comprehensive final exam

Objective and standardized. Cannot be purchased. 90%+ earns the Gold Seal. 80–89% earns HomeGuard Certified. The same bar for every contractor in the network.

03

Background and financial verification

Full background check on the contractor and all crew members who will enter your home. Financial verification completed before any credential is issued.

04

License and insurance confirmed

Current NJ HIC registration, minimum $1,000,000 general liability insurance, and NJ compliance bond all verified on file with HomeGuard before any listing is activated. Background check conducted and paid for by HomeGuard.

05

Ongoing performance monitoring

Certification is not a one-time event. A three-tier monitoring system — Advisory, Intervention, Suspension — tracks every contractor after credentialing. The Gold Seal can be revoked.

06

Annual recertification required

Every Gold Seal contractor renews annually. The credential reflects current standing — not just a passed exam from years ago.

The HomeGuard Standard

A standard that goes both ways.

Certified contractors commit to the HomeGuard Standard on every project — and so do the homeowners they work with. This is what makes the guarantee possible.

What Homeowners Commit To
  • ✓Milestone approvals completed within the defined window in the contract
  • ✓All scope changes requested in writing through the HomeGuard portal — no verbal requests
  • ✓Contractor-managed materials procurement on all projects — no homeowner-supplied materials
  • ✓Reasonable site access during defined working hours
  • ✓All communication with the contractor through the portal, not directly with crew
  • ✓Payment of verified completed milestones without withholding

When a homeowner says "I'll buy the materials myself and just pay for the labor," it sounds like a way to save money. In practice it never works. Homeowners miss items because they do not do this every day. Wrong quantities, wrong specifications, missing materials, delays waiting on a return trip to the supply house. And when something is discovered behind a wall — rot, old wiring, unexpected conditions — the material list changes immediately and the homeowner is not equipped to handle it. The project stalls. Everyone loses time and money. The HomeGuard Standard requires contractor-managed materials procurement on every project. It is not a restriction — it is what protects both the homeowner and the contractor.

Why This Matters

The bilateral standard is what makes the guarantee enforceable.

HomeGuard can stand behind every project because both sides of the project are accountable to the same standard. When homeowners fulfill their obligations, contractors can do their jobs. When contractors fulfill theirs, homeowners are protected. The standard works because it goes both ways.
